We benchmark the ODE baseline and the analytic HiPPO-SVGP on the solar stream under two timing protocols. In `kernel_only`, we measure only the construction of the interdomain kernel terms used by the variational objective. In `full_update`, we measure a complete variational update step consisting of ELBO evaluation, backpropagation, and one optimizer step. For the runtime-versus-$T$ study we fix the solar observation set and vary the effective stream length $T \in \{100, 300, 1000, 3000, 7000\}$ by adjusting the number of HiPPO discretization steps. For the runtime-versus-$M$ study we fix $T=1000$ and sweep the basis size $M \in \{8, 16, 32, 64, 128\}$. Across all runs, the solar data source, kernel family, random feature count ($R=1000$), hyperparameters, dtype, device, and random seed are kept fixed. Each point reports the median wall-clock runtime over 20 repeats after 5 warmup iterations, with CUDA synchronization immediately before and after each timing block.

The results demonstrate a clear speed advantage of the analytic method for long streams and small-to-moderate basis sizes. As $T$ increases from $100$ to $7000$ with $M=64$, the analytic kernel construction remains nearly constant (median `kernel_only` runtime from $126.8$ ms to $85.9$ ms), whereas the ODE baseline grows significantly (from $5.0$ ms to $281.1$ ms). Full ELBO update timings show the same trend. Consequently, the analytic implementation is most advantageous at large $T$, achieving roughly $3.3\times$ faster kernel construction and $3.2\times$ faster full update at $T=7000$.

For the $M$ sweep, the analytic method is faster at small $M$ (e.g., at $M=8$, median `kernel_only` runtime is $5.2$ ms vs $40.2$ ms for ODE; full update $10.7$ ms vs $42.8$ ms). As $M$ increases, the advantage diminishes, and by $M=64$ and $128$ the analytic method becomes slower due to the computational cost of spherical-Bessel and polynomial-order recursion. These measurements confirm that analytic kernel construction effectively removes the dominant dependence on stream length, while highlighting that its performance gain is most pronounced in long streams with small-to-moderate basis sizes.
